Overview
Executive Summary

Falls are the leading cause of injury-related death among adults over 65, killing one person every 19 minutes in the US alone. ZEROFALL is a passive, wearable-free floor-mat system that continuously analyzes an elderly person's gait patterns and alerts caregivers before a fall occurs — not after.

Unlike cameras (privacy-invasive), wearables (compliance-dependent), or reactive call buttons (too late), ZEROFALL sits invisibly beneath a standard doormat or bathroom mat. Pressure-sensitive sensors capture step cadence, weight distribution, stride asymmetry, and tremor — all without any action required from the user. A companion app delivers predictive alerts to family members and caregivers up to 72 hours before a high-risk event is likely to occur.

ZEROFALL is designed from the ground up around three non-negotiables: dignity (no cameras, no wearables), simplicity (plug-and-play, zero user interface), and accessibility (affordable enough for individual families, not just care facilities).

Section Three
Problem Statement

What is the problem?

Falls among adults aged 65 and older are a silent epidemic. Every year, approximately 36 million falls occur in the United States alone, resulting in 3 million emergency department visits, 800,000 hospitalizations, and over 32,000 deaths. Globally, falls are the second leading cause of accidental injury death, after road traffic injuries. The economic cost exceeds $80 billion annually in direct medical expenditure in the US — and that figure does not account for lost productivity, informal caregiving costs, or the psychological aftermath of fall-related trauma.

Critically, falls are not random events. Research consistently shows that deteriorating gait patterns — subtle changes in step cadence, stride symmetry, and weight transfer — are measurable predictors of fall risk that manifest days or even weeks before an actual fall occurs. Yet most interventions remain reactive: they activate only after someone has already fallen.

Who is affected?

  • Elderly individuals (65+): Primary sufferers. Falls cause physical injury, psychological trauma (post-fall syndrome, fear of walking), loss of independence, and are often the triggering event for care home admission.
  • Family caregivers: 53 million Americans serve as unpaid caregivers. Fall anxiety is cited as one of the top stressors, leading many to over-restrict their loved one's activities — paradoxically increasing fall risk through deconditioning.
  • Healthcare systems: Fall-related hospitalizations are a major driver of cost. Hip fracture alone costs an average of $35,000–$40,000 per event in the US.
  • Long-term care facilities: Falls are the #1 reported adverse event in nursing homes. Facilities face significant liability exposure and regulatory burden.

Why does it matter?

The global population of adults over 65 is expected to double from 727 million in 2020 to 1.5 billion by 2050. The fall problem will scale proportionally — unless we shift from reactive management to predictive prevention. Every fall that doesn't happen saves an average of $10,000 in direct healthcare costs, preserves independence, and prevents the psychological cascade that follows a serious fall event.

What are the current limitations & why do existing approaches fail?

Existing Solution Limitation Why It Fails
Emergency call buttons (Life Alert) Reactive only Activates after the fall. Requires user to be conscious and wearing device.
Wearable sensors (smartwatches) Compliance gap 68% of elderly non-adherence rate. Must be worn consistently to be useful.
In-home cameras Privacy violations Most families and elderly users refuse camera monitoring in bedrooms and bathrooms — where most falls occur.
Clinical gait assessment Point-in-time, infrequent Gait is assessed every 6–12 months. Deterioration between appointments goes undetected.
Grip bars & home modifications Environmental only Reduces fall severity but does not predict or prevent the fall trigger. High installation cost.

The Core Gap

No existing solution provides continuous, passive, predictive fall-risk monitoring that respects the dignity and privacy of the user while requiring zero behavioral change from the elderly person themselves. This is the gap ZEROFALL fills.

Section Four
Proposed Solution

What ZEROFALL Is

ZEROFALL is a passive gait-monitoring floor-mat system that continuously captures pressure-based walking data from an elderly person's daily movements — without any cameras, wearables, or active user participation. The system has three integrated components:

① The ZeroMat™ (Physical Sensor Mat)

A 90cm × 60cm floor mat embedding a 32×20 grid of piezoelectric pressure sensors. Placed at high-traffic locations: bedroom doorway, bathroom entrance, hallway. Washable, non-slip, aesthetically standard. Communicates via Bluetooth Low Energy to a hub. Battery life: 18 months. Retail target: $149 per mat.

② The ZeroHub™ (Local Processing Unit)

A small, plug-in hub (size of a phone charger) that aggregates data from up to 6 mats and runs a locally-stored gait analysis model. Uploads compressed gait trend data — not raw pressure data — to the cloud. This ensures privacy: only derived metrics (stride symmetry score, cadence variance, step hesitation index) leave the home. No audio or visual data. Retail target: $49.

③ The ZeroApp™ (Caregiver Intelligence Platform)

A mobile and web application for family members, professional caregivers, and healthcare providers. Delivers a daily "Gait Health Score" (1–100), trend graphs over time, and tiered alerts: Green (normal), Yellow (watch — subtle decline detected), Red (high risk — intervention recommended). Integrates with GP referral workflows in supported markets. Monthly subscription: $12/month.

How It Works — The Science

ZEROFALL's core insight is that gait is a vital sign. Research from MIT AgeLab, Mayo Clinic, and the New England Journal of Medicine has demonstrated that the following gait parameters deteriorate measurably before a fall occurs:

  • Step cadence variability: Healthy walkers have consistent step rhythm. Pre-fall gait shows increasing rhythm irregularity (coefficient of variation >3% signals risk).
  • Stride asymmetry: Left-right weight-bearing imbalance predicts neurological and musculoskeletal decline.
  • Heel-strike pressure: Reduction in heel pressure indicates shuffling — a strong fall predictor.
  • Step hesitation: Pausing before stepping onto a mat (threshold hesitation) correlates with fear of falling and cognitive decline.
  • Night-time gait frequency: Increased nocturnal movement (bathroom trips) correlates with fall risk, dehydration, and medication side effects.

The local model builds a personalized baseline for each user over the first 14 days, then continuously monitors for statistically significant deviation. This personalization is what makes ZEROFALL effective — it doesn't compare the user to a population norm, it compares them to their own healthy baseline.

Who Would Use It

  • Adult children of aging parents (primary purchaser) — living separately, seeking peace of mind without invasive monitoring
  • Home care agencies — deploying across client portfolios for predictive service escalation
  • Assisted living facilities — institutional licensing for shared common areas and private rooms
  • Health insurers — offering as a preventive benefit to reduce fall-related claims (strong ROI case)
  • NHS/public health systems — fall prevention programs with community distribution

Section Five
Innovation & Uniqueness
🛡️

Zero-Compliance Innovation

Unlike wearables or cameras, ZEROFALL requires no behavioral change from the elderly user. The mat is passive — it works automatically, every time someone walks across it, whether they remember to press a button or not.

🔒

Privacy-by-Architecture

No cameras. No microphones. No biometric data upload. Only compressed gait metrics leave the home. This is a fundamental design principle, not a feature — making adoption viable in markets where other solutions have been rejected on privacy grounds.

📊

Predictive vs. Reactive

Current solutions detect falls. ZEROFALL predicts them. The shift from reactive to predictive is the central innovation — enabled by continuous passive data collection that no previous form factor has achieved at consumer price points.

🧠

Personalized Baseline Model

The system adapts to each individual. A 68-year-old marathon runner and a 68-year-old with Parkinson's will have completely different baselines — and ZEROFALL correctly monitors each relative to their own norm, dramatically reducing false positives.

💰

Consumer-Accessible Price Point

Existing clinical gait analysis systems cost $50,000–$200,000. ZEROFALL achieves clinical-grade insight for $149 + $12/month — accessible to individual families, not just institutions.

♻️

Insurance-Aligned Business Model

The subscription model enables insurer partnerships where ZEROFALL is offered as a preventive benefit at zero cost to the user, paid by insurers who save $30–50 for every $1 invested in fall prevention.

What Makes This Different from Existing Patents & Products

Several companies have explored pressure-mat concepts (SmartFloor, Bodyguard), but none have achieved the combination of consumer price point + personalized predictive modeling + caregiver intelligence platform + privacy-preserving architecture. ZEROFALL is not a sensor product — it is a predictive health service delivered through a sensor product. The mat is the interface; the value is the prediction.

E. Process Flow Diagram

Step 1 — Passive Capture

Elderly person walks across ZeroMat during normal daily activity. 640 sensors capture a full pressure map at 100Hz — each footstep generates a rich biomechanical signature.

Step 2 — Local Feature Extraction (ZeroHub)

ZeroHub processes raw sensor data locally, extracting 12 gait features per step. Raw data is discarded; only features are stored.

Step 3 — Baseline Calibration (Days 1–14)

System establishes a personalized healthy-baseline gait profile using the first two weeks of data. No alerts are generated during calibration.

Step 4 — Continuous Anomaly Detection

From day 15 onward, a locally-running anomaly detection model compares each day's gait features to the individual baseline. Deviation scoring triggers tiered alerts.

Step 5 — Caregiver Alert Delivery

ZeroApp delivers push notifications and daily summary reports. Yellow alerts recommend scheduling a GP visit. Red alerts include recommended immediate actions and one-tap emergency contact.

Optional Demonstration

Demonstration Concept: "Eleanor's Week"

The demonstration would take the form of a 3-minute animated walkthrough following a fictional user — Eleanor, 78 — through one week of ZEROFALL monitoring. The animation would show:

  1. Day 1: Family installs the mat during a Sunday visit. Eleanor goes about her week normally, completely unaware of any monitoring.
  2. Day 4: ZEROFALL detects a 12% increase in stride asymmetry and a new hesitation pattern. Caregiver app score moves from Green to Yellow.
  3. Day 5: Sarah (daughter) receives a Yellow alert: "Eleanor's gait shows early signs of balance shift. Consider scheduling a GP visit."
  4. Day 7: GP visit reveals a new medication interaction affecting balance. Medication is adjusted. Gait score returns to baseline within 3 days.
  5. No fall ever occurs.

"ZEROFALL doesn't tell you about the fall. It tells you about the week before the fall — when there's still time to do something about it."
